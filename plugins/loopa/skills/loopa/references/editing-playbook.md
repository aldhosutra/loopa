# Editing playbook: what makes a short work

Research from Nov 2026 (mostly vendor and agency blogs, so treat the numbers as directional):
- Viewers decide within ~1.5–3 s. One source puts the first algorithmic decision at ~1.5 s, and Meta cites 1.7 s. ([opus.pro anatomy](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026), [opus.pro first 3 s](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-tiktoks-first-3-seconds-2026), [tlinky](https://tlinky.com/3-second-hook/))
- A strong hook combines three layers: first visual, on-screen text, and sound or voice. Leading with the outcome or transformation works. Curiosity gaps and specific claims help.
- Animated, burned-in captions on every video; 9:16 vertical. ([jetfuel](https://jetfuel.agency/short-form-video-optimization-2026/))
- Food and hands-only formats that keep showing up: faceless hands-on content, ASMR, cinematic slow-mo, velocity edits with speed ramps, fast cuts on the beat. ([opus.pro aesthetics](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026))

Re-run a quick search each time. This list ages.

## Hooks that worked

- **Result first + bold claim**: open on the most appetizing frame, then slam a claim box onto it ("TANPA NASI"), add an emoji, and shake on the slam.
- **POV relatable**: "POV: lagi diet" / "tapi ngidam nasi goreng 😭" over the most kinetic slow-mo shot.
- **Curiosity / denial**: "ini bukan nasi." over a close-up that looks like something else. The payoff comes at the end with "ini OAT."
- The first frame must be sharp. If the best-looking shot drifts out of focus, use its sharp part.

## Structure

- Hook (1.5–2.5 s), then a fast middle (0.5–1.5 s per shot, cuts on beats), then a build (drums out, riser), then the reveal (impact, flash, payoff text), then a CTA/tail (save, comment keyword, "simpan dulu") that loops into the hook.
- Retention devices: numbered step chips, an ingredient list that builds up, a freeze-frame with a red circle callout, a progress bar, and a story told across several captions.
- CTA ideas: "SIMPAN dulu, masak besok pagi 📌", "mau resepnya? komen "OAT" 👇", "save & coba sendiri".

## Text

- Bold sans (Poppins Black/ExtraBold) with a soft shadow, plus keyword highlight boxes in yellow `(255,214,10)` with dark text. Or TikTok-native white text with a black stroke of 8–14 px. Or an editorial serif italic (Fraunces) for cinematic cuts.
- Sizes on 1080 px wide: headline 108–150, slam word 190–280, captions 64–84, chips 46–64. Anything over ~16 characters at 74 px should be two lines.
- Safe zone: y 0.15–0.40 for main text. Karaoke captions at y≈0.68–0.70. Avoid the bottom ~22% and the right ~12%, where the like and comment rail sits.

## Visual

- Different grade per video: warm punchy (sat ~1.2), soft film (lift + grain), or teal-orange high contrast.
- Every cut gets a zoom punch. Some cuts get whip blur. Hits get an exposure pop and shake. Reveals get a white flash.
- Slow-mo on action hits (drops, pours, egg) at 0.25–0.5x with optical flow. Timelapse for stirring (5–9x).

## Sound

- The audio of raw cooking footage is quiet ambience, so the sound has to be designed.
- Give each video its own genre: upbeat pluck/house 120 BPM, lo-fi 96 BPM with a tape-stop drop, phonk 140 BPM.
- Layer whooshes on cuts, pops on text, a riser into the reveal, impact + bell on the reveal, and the varispeed natural sizzle underneath.
- Target about -14 LUFS integrated.
- Mention to the user that they can swap in a trending sound in-app. Platform-native trending audio can help reach, and the non-dubbed versions suit that.
